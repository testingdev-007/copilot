Level 1 · Summarise

A summary helps one person, once.

3 min

Summaries are a great start. But you read it, nod, and it disappears. The trick is knowing which rung of the ladder you're on. Sort six real tasks onto the right rung to earn your first XP.

Rung Sorter

Rung cheat-sheet
  • Summarise: just for you, one-off.
  • Transform: same information reshaped for other audiences.
  • Template: a reusable prompt with gaps anyone can fill.
  • Tool: something people click, no prompting needed.
  • Process: an agent that works without you in the room.

Level 2 · Transform

One source, many audiences

5 min

Take a messy staff survey about a new expenses system and turn it into something three different audiences can use. First, play detective: the data has four kinds of mess hiding in it.

Mess DetectiveClick one example of each kind of mess in the table.
Mixed date formatsA score that isn't 1 to 5A blank commentA duplicate row

Three prompts, in order

A · Clean 
Clean this survey data. Make all dates DD/MM/YYYY, turn scores into whole numbers 1 to 5, remove exact duplicate rows and flag any blank comments. Tell me what you changed.
B · Find themes 
Add a Theme column. Group each comment into one of 5 to 6 themes. Then make a table of theme counts with average score, sorted by most mentioned.
C · Three audiences 
Using these themes, write three versions: (1) a 3-sentence note for the executive team, (2) a bulleted action list for the project team, (3) a friendly "You said, we're doing" message for all staff. No jargon.
Audience Match

The shift: you didn't write any of those messages. You described the audience and the shape. Start every request with "for [audience]".
